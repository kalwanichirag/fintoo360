Tech leads global equities higher; Treasury yields climb. Your MATCAP briefing for 28 September 2026.
Equities rose across most major markets even as the US bond selloff deepened, with the 10 year Treasury yield reaching its highest level since 2007. Technology led the gains, helped by a sharp rally in Meta, while Asian chip markets extended their run with the KOSPI up 2.7% and the Nikkei up 2.1%. Micron’s fiscal fourth quarter results on Wednesday, 30 September, will be a key test of whether the technology rally continues. In Europe, the STOXX 600 rose 0.5%, its first weekly gain after three consecutive weekly losses, as oil eased late in the week on hopes of a phased US and Iran agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. The Hang Seng fell 1.0%, and the NIFTY 50 lost 0.9% for a seventh straight weekly decline, its longest losing run since 2020.

Global Economic News
Treasury yields reach 2007 highs:
A week after the Federal Reserve’s first rate increase in three years, bond yields moved higher still. A flash S&P Global PMI showing US business activity at its strongest in more than five years, together with weak demand at a 5 year Treasury auction, lifted the 5 year yield above 5% for the first time since 2007. The 10 year closed at 5.17% and the 30 year near 5.49%, a level last seen in 2004. Fed Governor Michael Barr said his base case is for several more increases, and markets ended the week pricing roughly a two in three chance of a hike in October.
US and China extend trade truce to 10 January:
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced a two month extension of the Busan truce, which had been due to expire in November, as Xi Jinping arrived in Washington for his first state visit in 11 years. The truce keeps US tariffs lower and suspends Chinese export controls on rare earths and critical minerals. The three day visit produced few concrete agreements, and analysts read the short extension as Washington keeping leverage in negotiations.
Oil eases on Hormuz talks, India extends losing streak:
Crude prices fell on Friday after reports that negotiators are exploring a phased path in which Tehran reopens the Strait of Hormuz and Washington lifts its blockade. Brent settled at USD 104.32 per barrel and WTI at USD 92.41. Elevated crude and higher global yields kept pressure on India, where foreign investors sold INR 12,609 crore of equities in September through Thursday, taking 2026 outflows to about INR 2.37 lakh crore. The NIFTY 50 fell 1.6% on Thursday, its weakest session since March.
Company Specific News
META
Weekly gain of about 13%
Meta Platforms
Shares rose about 13% over the week, including an 11% gain on Monday, the stock’s best day since April 2025, after its Muse AI agent reached the top of Apple’s App Store. Investors see Muse, which can book travel, manage email and complete purchases for users, as a source of revenue beyond advertising. The stock eased on Friday on reports of computing strain from rapid user growth, and Amazon has blocked Muse from its marketplace.
MSFT
Friday gain of 3.7%
Microsoft
Shares rose 3.7% on Friday, the best performance in the Dow, after Microsoft relaunched Copilot as a single app focused entirely on enterprise customers, combining its consumer and workplace assistants and stepping back from a personal AI companion. The new app adds a coding tool built on GitHub Copilot and Autopilot, a persistent agent that handles complex tasks in the background, with pay as you go billing for agent usage. Oppenheimer raised its price target to USD 570 from USD 515, citing customers standardising on Microsoft as their primary enterprise AI platform.
UBS
Shares gained as much as 4% on Friday
UBS Group
UBS led European banks higher on Friday after Semafor reported that the Swiss lender has revived discussions on moving out from under Swiss regulators, including a possible combination with a foreign bank, as it faces stricter domestic capital rules. The shares gained as much as 4% during the session, while Morgan Stanley declined on the report. Third quarter results are due on 28 October.
