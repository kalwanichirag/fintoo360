Global equities ended the week broadly lower as long-dated government bond yields hit multi-decade highs and firm oil prices kept inflation concerns in focus. The S&P 500 fell 1.4% and the Nasdaq Composite 2.1%, snapping a three-week run, while the Nikkei 225 shed 3.9% and European benchmarks were mostly softer. Hong Kong bucked the trend, with the Hang Seng Index up 3.6%, and healthcare outperformed globally after Moderna and Merck reported positive late-stage cancer vaccine data.

Global Economic News
Global bond sell-off pushes yields to multi-decade highs:
The 30-year U.S. Treasury yield rose above 5.2%, its highest in close to two decades, and the 10-year reached roughly 4.70%, with Germany’s 10-year Bund at 3.22%, a level last seen in 2011. The Treasury’s move to at least double buybacks of 10- to 30-year debt brought only temporary relief.
July FOMC minutes signal a hawkish tilt:
Minutes released on Wednesday showed Federal Reserve officials see scope for higher rates if inflation fails to ease, with three policymakers dissenting in favour of a July hike. Futures cut the implied odds of a September cut to about 33% and moved to price a hike by December.
U.S.–Iran tensions keep oil elevated:
Crude posted a weekly gain, with Brent near $89 a barrel, after Washington pledged tougher sanctions on Tehran and a possible naval response over access through the Strait of Hormuz. Offsetting this, Friday’s preliminary S&P Global Composite PMI came in at 56.04, the highest since March 2022.
Company Specific News
MRNA
First Phase 3 win for an mRNA cancer vaccine
Moderna surges 174% on first Phase 3 win for an mRNA cancer vaccine:
Moderna and Merck said intismeran, a personalised mRNA vaccine given with Keytruda, met both endpoints in a trial of more than 1,000 high-risk melanoma patients, reducing recurrence and spread. Moderna closed Wednesday up roughly 177% at $174.38, and RBC Capital raised its price target to $130 from $45. An FDA submission is planned, with further oncology readouts due over the next one to two years; Merck and Pfizer also gained.
DE
Full-year net income: $4.75–$5.0 billion
Deere beats on third-quarter earnings and lifts full-year outlook:
Net income rose to $1.38 billion from $1.29 billion a year earlier, on revenues up 5% to $12.6 billion. Construction and forestry sales jumped 18% and small agriculture and turf 12%, offsetting a 6% decline in production and precision agriculture. Full-year net income is guided to $4.75–$5.0 billion.
KR TECH
SK Hynix almost 13% • Samsung Electronics 9.5%
Korean memory names spark a one-day Asian tech rally:
SK Hynix rose almost 13% and Samsung Electronics 9.5% on Thursday on renewed AI-driven memory demand, lifting the KOSPI 5.9% and triggering a buy-side sidecar on the Korea Exchange. The move pared but did not reverse the index’s weekly loss.
