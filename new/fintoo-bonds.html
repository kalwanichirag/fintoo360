What is a bond, and how does it work?

When you buy a bond, you lend money to the issuer, such as the Government of India, a state, a public-sector company or a private company. The issuer promises to pay you interest on fixed dates and return your money at maturity. Unlike shares, you do not own part of the business; you are its lender.

Listed bonds sit in your demat account like shares, and you can sell them on the stock exchange before maturity, though trading can be thin. Your return is known in advance if you hold to maturity and the issuer pays as promised.

Example: a five-year bond with an 8% annual coupon, held to maturity.

Six terms worth knowing

Face value
The amount the issuer repays at maturity, now often ₹10,000 per bond.
Coupon
The interest rate on the face value, paid monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or yearly.
Maturity
The date your money is repaid. Some bonds can be called back earlier by the issuer.
Yield to maturity (YTM)
Your actual yearly return if you buy at today's price and hold until maturity.
Credit rating
An agency's view of how likely the issuer is to pay on time, from AAA down to D.
Secured or unsecured
Whether specific assets back the bond if the issuer cannot pay.

Why invest in bonds, and what are the risks?

Bonds give you predictable income and help protect your capital. But a bond is only as safe as the borrower behind it. Here is the good and the not-so-good, at a glance.

+Why people invest

  • Predictable income

    Interest arrives on fixed dates you know in advance.

  • Capital protection

    High-quality bonds return your money at maturity.

  • Often more than FDs

    Good-quality corporate bonds usually yield more than bank deposits.

  • Balances equity

    Bonds steady a portfolio when share prices fall.

  • Lock in today's rate

    Your coupon stays fixed even if interest rates fall later.

  • Start from ₹10,000

    Many corporate bonds now come in ₹10,000 units.

!Risks to keep in mind

  • The borrower may not payLower-rated issuers can delay or default. That is credit risk.
  • Prices move with ratesIf rates rise, your bond's price falls before maturity.
  • Hard to sell quicklyMany bonds trade rarely, so selling early can cost you.
  • Not insured like FDsBonds have no deposit insurance cover.

The fix for most of these: stick to strong ratings, spread across issuers, and match maturity to when you need the money.

Fixed vs floating rate

A fixed-rate bond pays the same coupon throughout. A floating-rate bond resets its coupon to a benchmark, so income rises and falls with rates.

Secured vs unsecured

Secured bonds are backed by specific assets of the issuer. Unsecured and subordinated bonds rank lower if the issuer runs into trouble.

Primary vs secondary market

In the primary market you buy a new issue directly from the issuer. In the secondary market you buy an existing bond from another investor on the exchange.

Why do bond prices fall when interest rates rise?

Because new bonds start paying the higher rate, so older bonds with lower coupons become less attractive and their price drops until their yield matches the market. The reverse happens when rates fall. If you hold to maturity, these swings do not matter: you still get every coupon and your full face value.

  • Longer bonds move more. A 10-year bond's price swings far more than a 1-year bond's for the same change in rates.
  • Where rates stand now. India's 10-year government bond yield was about 7.15% at the end of September 2026, with the RBI's repo rate at 5.25%.

What do bond credit ratings mean?

A credit rating is an independent opinion on how likely the issuer is to pay interest and principal on time. SEBI-registered agencies such as CRISIL, ICRA, CARE and India Ratings assign them. AAA is the safest; anything below BBB- is below investment grade. A rating is not a guarantee, and it can change.

Bonds vs fixed deposits vs debt mutual funds: which is better?

Each suits a different need. FDs are simplest and insured up to ₹5 lakh per bank. Bonds let you lock a specific rate and maturity. Debt funds give instant diversification and easy exit, but no fixed maturity value. Many investors use all three.

How are bonds taxed in India?

There are two parts. Interest is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate every year. If you sell before maturity, the gain is a capital gain, taxed by how long you held the bond and whether it is listed.

Common bond investing mistakes, and what to do instead

Most bond losses come from chasing yield without checking who is borrowing. A few simple habits avoid them.

  • 01

    Picking the highest coupon

    Instead: ask why it pays more. A much higher rate usually means much higher risk.

  • 02

    Putting everything in one issuer

    Instead: spread across several issuers and sectors, so one default cannot hurt badly.

  • 03

    Ignoring the fine print

    Instead: check whether the bond is secured, subordinated, perpetual or callable before you buy.

  • 04

    Buying long bonds for short goals

    Instead: match maturity to when you need the money, so you never have to sell early at a loss.

  • 05

    Comparing coupons, not yields

    Instead: compare yield to maturity, which accounts for the price you actually pay.

  • 06

    Forgetting the tax

    Instead: compare returns after tax. At 30%, a 9% coupon nets about 6.3%.

Bond FAQs

Clear answers to the questions investors ask before buying their first bond.

What is a bond?

A bond is a loan you give to a government, public-sector body or company. In return, the issuer pays you interest, called the coupon, at fixed intervals and repays the face value on a set maturity date. Bonds are used for predictable income and to protect capital.

What are the different types of bonds in India?

The main types are government securities and treasury bills, state development loans, PSU and bank bonds, corporate bonds and NCDs, tax-free bonds, 54EC capital gains bonds, RBI floating rate savings bonds, zero-coupon bonds and municipal bonds. They differ mainly in who borrows, how safe they are and how they are taxed.

Are bonds safe investments?

Bonds issued by the Government of India carry the lowest credit risk. Corporate bonds are only as safe as the company behind them, which is why credit ratings matter. Bonds are not insured like bank deposits, and their market price can fall if interest rates rise, though this does not matter if you hold to maturity.

What is the coupon rate of a bond?

The coupon rate is the yearly interest the issuer pays, as a percentage of the bond's face value. A ₹10,000 bond with an 8% coupon pays ₹800 a year, either in one payment or split monthly, quarterly or half-yearly.

What is yield to maturity (YTM)?

Yield to maturity is your actual yearly return if you buy a bond at today's market price and hold it until it matures, assuming every payment is made. It differs from the coupon when you buy above or below face value, which is why YTM is the better number for comparing bonds.

Why do bond prices fall when interest rates rise?

When rates rise, new bonds pay more, so existing bonds with lower coupons become less attractive and their prices drop until their yields match the market. When rates fall, existing bond prices rise. Longer-maturity bonds react the most.

Are bonds better than fixed deposits?

Not always. Good-quality bonds often yield more than FDs and let you lock a rate for longer, and listed bonds held over a year can get lower capital gains tax. FDs are simpler, have no market price movement and are insured up to ₹5 lakh per bank. Many investors hold both.

How can I buy bonds in India?

You can buy government securities through the RBI Retail Direct portal, and corporate and PSU bonds through SEBI-registered online bond platforms or a stockbroker, either at a new issue or on the exchange. Bonds are held in your demat account. A Fintoo advisor can help you choose which bonds to buy.

What is the minimum investment in bonds?

Since 2024, SEBI allows most corporate bonds to be issued with a face value of ₹10,000, down from ₹1 lakh earlier and ₹10 lakh before that. Government securities through RBI Retail Direct can also be bought in small amounts.

What is the difference between the primary and secondary bond market?

In the primary market you buy newly issued bonds directly from the issuer, at face value. In the secondary market you buy existing bonds from other investors on the stock exchange, at a price that may be above or below face value depending on interest rates and demand.

What is the difference between fixed-rate and floating-rate bonds?

A fixed-rate bond pays the same coupon for its whole life. A floating-rate bond resets its coupon periodically to a benchmark, such as a government bond yield or the NSC rate, so your income rises when rates rise and falls when they fall.

How is bond interest taxed in India?

Bond interest is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate. Listed bonds sold after 12 months attract 12.5% tax on the gain, and within 12 months, your slab rate. Unlisted bonds are taxed at slab rate whatever the holding period. TDS of 10% applies to listed bond interest above ₹10,000 a year.

What are tax-free bonds?

Tax-free bonds were issued by government-backed bodies such as NHAI, REC, PFC and IRFC, and their interest is fully exempt from income tax. There have been no new issues for several years, but existing bonds can be bought on the stock exchange. They suit investors in high tax brackets.

What are 54EC bonds?

54EC bonds let you save capital gains tax on the sale of land or a building by investing the long-term gain, up to ₹50 lakh, within six months of the sale. They are issued by bodies such as REC, PFC and IRFC, carry a five-year lock-in and pay taxable interest.

What is a bond index?

A bond index tracks the prices and returns of a defined group of bonds, such as government securities of a certain maturity or AAA corporate bonds. It is used to measure how a part of the bond market is performing and as the benchmark for bond funds and target maturity funds.
