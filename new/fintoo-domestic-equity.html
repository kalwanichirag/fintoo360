What is equity investing, and how does the share market work?

Equity investing means buying shares of companies listed on the NSE or BSE. You profit in two ways: the share price rises as the company grows, and the company may pay part of its profit as dividends. Trades go through a SEBI-registered broker, and shares are held in your own demat account.

Indian markets are open from 9:15 am to 3:30 pm, Monday to Friday, except exchange holidays. Shares you buy reach your demat account the next working day, under India's T+1 settlement cycle.

Six terms worth knowing

Market capitalisation
Share price × number of shares: the market's value of the whole company.
P/E ratio
Price divided by earnings per share: how many years of profit you are paying for.
Dividend
A share of profit paid to shareholders, usually once or twice a year.
Blue-chip stock
A large, well-established company with a long record of steady profits.
Index
A basket that tracks the market, such as the Nifty 50, Sensex or Nifty 500.
Demat account
An electronic account, held with NSDL or CDSL, where your shares are kept.

Why invest in stocks, and what are the risks?

Shares let you own a slice of India's growing companies. But no return is guaranteed, and the path is bumpy. Here is the good and the not-so-good, at a glance.

+Why people invest

  • Long-term growth

    Equity has historically beaten inflation over long periods.

  • Real ownership

    You own the business, with voting rights and a share of profits.

  • Easy to buy and sell

    Large stocks trade every second the market is open.

  • No ongoing fund fees

    Direct shares carry no expense ratio, only trading costs.

  • Dividends

    Many mature companies share profits as regular cash payouts.

  • Low long-term tax

    12.5% on gains after a year, with ₹1.25 lakh a year tax-free.

!Risks to keep in mind

  • Prices swing sharplyMarkets can fall 30% or more in a bad year.
  • One company can failA single stock can lose most of its value.
  • Too much in one placeA few stocks or one sector can sink a portfolio.
  • Emotions get in the wayBuying on hype and selling in panic hurts returns most.

The fix for most of these: own quality companies, spread across sectors, and give them time.

What are large cap, mid cap and small cap stocks?

SEBI ranks every listed company by its market value. The top 100 are large caps, the next 150 are mid caps, and the rest are small caps. Bigger companies are usually steadier; smaller ones can grow faster but fall harder. AMFI updates the list every six months.

Growth vs value stocks

Growth stocks are companies expected to grow profits faster than the market, often at higher valuations. Value stocks trade below what their profits or assets seem to justify. Most good portfolios hold both.

Sectors matter too

Banks, IT, consumer, healthcare and industrials react differently to interest rates, exports and the economy. Spreading across sectors smooths the ride.

How do you judge whether a stock is worth buying?

Ask two questions: is this a good business, and is the price sensible? A great company bought at a very high price can still be a poor investment. These checks cover most of what matters.

Direct stocks vs equity mutual funds: which suits you?

Both give you equity exposure. Mutual funds suit most people starting out; a direct stock portfolio suits larger investors who want more control, transparency and a focused set of companies. Many HNI portfolios hold both.

How are shares taxed in India?

You pay tax when you sell at a profit and when you receive dividends. Holding listed shares for more than 12 months cuts the tax on gains to 12.5%, and the first ₹1.25 lakh of long-term gains each year is tax-free.

Common stock market mistakes, and what to do instead

Most investors lose money in shares not because of the market, but because of their own decisions. A few habits avoid the worst of it.

  • 01

    Acting on tips and social media

    Instead: buy only what you understand, backed by the company's own numbers.

  • 02

    Betting big on one stock

    Instead: cap any single stock at around 10% and any sector at around 20%.

  • 03

    Averaging down on a broken business

    Instead: re-check why you bought it. Add more only if the business is still sound.

  • 04

    Trading too often

    Instead: hold quality companies for years. Every trade costs brokerage, STT and tax.

  • 05

    Chasing cheap-looking penny stocks

    Instead: a low share price is not cheap. Judge the business and its valuation.

  • 06

    Using money you need soon

    Instead: invest in shares only what you will not need for five to seven years.

Stock market FAQs

Clear answers to the questions investors ask before buying their first shares.

What is equity investing?

Equity investing means buying shares of companies listed on a stock exchange such as the NSE or BSE. Each share makes you a part-owner of the company. You can earn from a rise in the share price as the business grows, and from dividends the company pays out of its profits.

Is investing in the stock market safe?

The Indian market is well regulated by SEBI, trades settle through clearing corporations, and your shares are held in your own demat account. But share prices can fall sharply, and individual companies can fail. Investing in quality companies, spreading across sectors and staying invested for years reduces the risk, but does not remove it.

How do I start investing in shares in India?

Open a demat and trading account with a SEBI-registered stockbroker using your PAN and Aadhaar, link your bank account, and place buy orders through the broker's app. Before buying, decide how much of your money belongs in equity and which companies fit your goals, ideally with a SEBI-registered investment adviser.

What is market capitalisation?

Market capitalisation is the total market value of a company: its share price multiplied by the number of shares. SEBI uses it to classify stocks: the top 100 companies are large caps, ranks 101 to 250 are mid caps, and the rest are small caps. AMFI updates the list every six months.

What is a blue-chip stock?

A blue-chip stock is a share in a large, well-established company with a long record of steady profits, strong finances and often regular dividends. In India, most blue chips are large-cap companies in indices such as the Nifty 50 and Sensex.

What is the P/E ratio?

The price-to-earnings ratio is the share price divided by the company's earnings per share. It shows how many years of current profit you are paying for. A high P/E can mean the market expects strong growth, or that the share is expensive; it is best compared with the company's own history and its peers.

What is the difference between growth and value stocks?

Growth stocks are companies expected to grow profits faster than the market, and they often trade at higher valuations. Value stocks trade below what their profits, cash flows or assets seem to justify, and may offer higher returns if the market re-rates them. Many portfolios hold both.

Is the share market open on weekends?

No. NSE and BSE are open from 9:15 am to 3:30 pm, Monday to Friday, and closed on weekends and exchange holidays. The exchanges occasionally hold a special session, such as Muhurat trading on Diwali, which can fall on a weekend.

How much money do I need to start investing in stocks?

You can buy a single share, so the minimum is the price of one share plus small charges. For a properly diversified direct stock portfolio of 15 to 25 companies, a larger amount makes more sense; with smaller sums, equity mutual funds usually give better diversification.

How are shares taxed in India?

Gains on listed shares sold within 12 months are taxed at 20%. Gains on shares held longer are taxed at 12.5%, with the first ₹1.25 lakh of long-term gains each year exempt. Dividends are taxed at your slab rate, with 10% TDS above ₹10,000 a year per company. STT of 0.1% applies on delivery trades.

Should I invest in stocks directly or through mutual funds?

Equity mutual funds suit most investors, especially with smaller amounts or little time, because they offer instant diversification and professional management. A direct stock portfolio can suit larger investors who want control, full transparency, no expense ratio and the ability to time their own gains and losses for tax.

How many stocks should I own?

For a direct equity portfolio, 15 to 25 stocks across different sectors usually gives enough diversification while keeping each holding meaningful. Fewer than ten makes the portfolio risky; many more than 30 makes it hard to track and starts to look like an index fund.

What is a stop-loss?

A stop-loss is a pre-set level at which you sell to limit a loss. It can apply to a single stock or, as in Fintoo's approach, to the portfolio as a whole. It protects against large drawdowns, but in fast-moving markets the sale may happen below the set level.

What is T+1 settlement?

T+1 means a trade settles one working day after it is made: shares you buy reach your demat account, and money from shares you sell reaches you, on the next working day. India moved to T+1 for all stocks in January 2023.
