What is investment planning, and why do HNIs need it?

Investment planning is building a personal strategy for your money: your goals, your risk profile, the mix of assets that fits them, and the specific investments that deliver it. For HNIs the problem is rarely too little choice. It is too many products, sold separately, that never add up to one portfolio.

  • Portfolios grow by accident

    Funds bought over the years from different banks and platforms often overlap heavily, so ten funds can behave like three while you pay for all ten.

  • Wealth sits in one place

    A business, ESOPs, property or idle cash can dominate an HNI balance sheet. Concentration is the biggest risk most portfolios carry.

  • The choice of vehicle matters

    The same strategy can be held through a mutual fund, SIF, PMS or AIF, each with a different cost, tax treatment, lock-in and minimum. Picking wrongly can cost more than picking the wrong fund.

  • Tax changes the real return

    Since 2023, debt funds and market-linked debentures are taxed at your slab rate. For someone in the top bracket, the post-tax return is what counts.

  • Advice should be separate from selling

    SEBI's rules for registered investment advisers separate advice from product distribution. That lets your plan be built on what fits you, often in lower-cost direct plans, rather than on which product pays the highest commission.

      PMS vs AIF vs mutual fund vs SIF: which is right for you?

      None is better in general. Mutual funds are the low-cost, liquid default; SIFs add specialised strategies from ₹10 lakh; PMS gives a concentrated portfolio in your own name from ₹50 lakh; AIFs reach private and alternative assets from ₹1 crore. The right mix depends on size, tax and how long you can lock money in.

      How are investments taxed in India?

      Long-term gains on listed shares and equity funds are taxed at 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year, and short-term gains at 20%. Interest, and gains on debt funds bought on or after 1 April 2023 and on market-linked debentures, are taxed at your slab rate. For HNIs in the top bracket, that difference shapes the whole portfolio.

      From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act. Section numbers changed; the capital gains rates below did not.

      Investment planning FAQs

      Straight answers to the questions HNIs ask us most.

      Last reviewed 28 September 2026 by [Advisor name, credential], Fintoo.

      What is investment planning?

      Investment planning is building a personal strategy for investing your money to reach one or more goals. It covers setting the goals, understanding your risk profile, deciding how much goes into each asset class, choosing the specific investments, and reviewing them over time. For HNIs it also covers tax, concentration, liquidity and which investment vehicles to use.

      Why do HNIs need investment planning?

      Because the main risks for HNIs are rarely too little choice. They are overlapping funds bought from different sellers, too much wealth in one business, stock or property, idle cash, and high post-tax costs. An investment plan puts everything into one allocation with a clear purpose for each holding.

      How should I invest ₹1 crore in India?

      Start with an emergency reserve and your goals, then set an asset allocation that fits your horizon and risk. At ₹1 crore, a focused set of six to eight low-cost mutual funds across Indian and global equity, debt and gold usually beats more complex products. A SIF can add a specialised strategy from ₹10 lakh.

      What is the minimum investment for PMS, AIF and SIF in India?

      SEBI sets the minimum at ₹50 lakh for portfolio management services (PMS), ₹1 crore for alternative investment funds (AIFs), with lower limits for accredited investors, and ₹10 lakh for specialised investment funds (SIFs). Individual products may set higher minimums.

      PMS vs AIF vs mutual fund: which is better?

      None is better in general. Mutual funds are the low-cost, liquid default. PMS holds a concentrated portfolio directly in your name from ₹50 lakh, but you pay tax on each trade. AIFs give access to private equity, private credit and other alternatives from ₹1 crore, usually with multi-year lock-ins. Most HNI portfolios use a mutual fund core with PMS or AIFs as satellites.

      What is a Specialised Investment Fund (SIF)?

      A SIF is a SEBI-regulated fund category run by mutual fund houses, with a minimum investment of ₹10 lakh. It can use strategies ordinary mutual funds cannot, such as long-short equity, and is taxed like a mutual fund. It sits between mutual funds and PMS or AIFs.

      How can I invest in US stocks from India?

      Resident Indians can invest abroad under the RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme, up to USD 250,000 per person each financial year, through foreign brokerages, international mutual funds, or GIFT City funds. Tax collected at source may apply on remittances above ₹10 lakh a year and can be adjusted against your income tax.

      Are market-linked debentures still worth investing in?

      For most investors, less than before. Since 1 April 2023, all gains on market-linked debentures are treated as short-term capital gains and taxed at your slab rate, however long you hold them. For someone in the 30% bracket, that removes much of their earlier advantage.

      Is it safe to buy unlisted or pre-IPO shares?

      It carries real risks: limited information, uncertain valuations, and difficulty selling. In June 2026 SEBI again warned investors against buying unlisted securities on unauthorised platforms, which offer no investor protection or dispute resolution. If you invest, keep it a small part of your portfolio and use regulated routes only.

      What is a good asset allocation for an HNI in India?

      There is no universal answer. A long horizon and high tolerance for volatility support more equity; near-term cash needs and low tolerance call for more debt. Many HNI portfolios end up with a diversified equity core, a debt allocation for stability and income, and smaller allocations to real estate, alternatives, global equity and gold.

      How often should I rebalance my portfolio?

      Review at least once a year, and rebalance when an asset class drifts meaningfully from its target, often by five percentage points or more. Rebalancing should be tax-aware, using new money, dividends and the annual ₹1.25 lakh equity gains exemption where possible.

      Why use a SEBI-registered investment adviser?

      A SEBI-registered investment adviser is regulated to give advice in your interest, and SEBI's rules separate advice from product distribution. That lets recommendations be made on merit, often in lower-cost direct plans. You can verify any adviser's registration on SEBI's website. Fintoo's registration number is INA000020031.
