In short

Insurance planning is working out which financial risks your family cannot absorb, and transferring them to an insurer at the lowest sensible cost. For most HNI families that means pure term life cover sized to your needs, your own health cover with a super top-up, and cover for disability, critical illness and property, kept separate from investing.

What is insurance planning, and why do HNIs need it?

Insurance planning identifies the events that could derail your family's finances, such as a death, a serious illness, a disability or a large liability, and decides which to insure and for how much. Wealth reduces some risks but raises others: a bigger lifestyle to replace, bigger loans, a business to protect and more assets to lose.

It is also about what not to buy. Many families pay large premiums for policies that mix insurance with low-return savings, while their real cover stays far too small.

  • Premature death

    Your family loses your income but keeps the expenses, loans and goals. Covered by term life insurance.

  • Hospitalisation

    Treatment at a top hospital can cost lakhs within days. Covered by your own health policy and a super top-up.

  • Critical illness

    Cancer, heart attack or stroke can stop you working for months. A critical illness plan pays a lump sum on diagnosis.

  • Disability

    Losing the ability to work can cost more than death, since expenses continue. Covered by personal accident and disability cover.

  • Property and belongings

    Fire, flood, theft and damage to your home, its contents, jewellery and art. Covered by home insurance.

  • Liability and business risk

    Claims against you as a director, professional or employer, and the loss of a key partner. Covered by liability and key-person cover.

Which types of insurance does a family need?

Almost every family with dependants needs term life insurance and its own health insurance. Critical illness, accident and home cover close the next biggest gaps. Business owners and directors should add key-person and liability cover. The table shows what each does and how it is usually sized.

Term insurance vs endowment and ULIPs: which is better?

For protection, term insurance wins. It gives the largest cover for the premium because it has no savings element. Buying term cover and investing the difference separately usually leaves a family with more cover and more flexibility than endowment, money-back or ULIP plans that try to do both.

Tax has also narrowed the gap. Maturity proceeds of traditional policies issued from 1 April 2023 are taxable if your total premiums on such policies exceed ₹5 lakh a year; for ULIPs issued from February 2021 the limit is ₹2.5 lakh.

How much health insurance does an HNI family need?

Enough to cover a major illness at the hospital you would actually choose, without selling investments. For metro families that often means a base family floater of ₹25 lakh or more, plus a super top-up of ₹50 lakh to ₹1 crore, held in your own name so it continues if you change jobs or retire. The fine print matters as much as the amount.

How a base policy and super top-up work together

₹1.25 crore of effective cover for much less than the premium of a single ₹1.25 crore policy. A super top-up counts all bills in the year towards the deductible; an ordinary top-up counts each claim separately.

Features to check before you buy

  • No room-rent limit

    Caps on room rent can cut the whole bill proportionately.

  • No co-payment

    A co-pay makes you share every claim, often 10 to 20%.

  • Short waiting periods

    Pre-existing conditions can wait up to three years; shorter is better.

  • Restoration of cover

    Refills the sum insured if it is used up during the year.

  • Consumables covered

    Gloves, kits and other items that are otherwise excluded add up.

  • Global or wide network

    Cashless at the hospitals you prefer, and treatment abroad if you want it.

Insurance planning for HNIs, business owners and NRIs

At higher wealth, the question shifts from "how much cover" to "who receives it, who can claim against it, and what it protects". These tools matter most.

MWP Act policies

A term policy bought by a married man under Section 6 of the Married Women's Property Act, 1874 is held in trust for his wife and children. Creditors and other relatives cannot claim the payout, which matters for anyone with business loans or guarantees.

Key-person insurance

The company insures a founder or key executive, so it can survive the loss: repaying loans, hiring a replacement and reassuring lenders and clients.

Partnership buy-sell cover

Partners insure each other so the surviving partners can buy a deceased partner's share at a fair price, and the family receives cash rather than a stake it cannot run.

High sum assured underwriting

Cover of several crores needs medical tests and proof of income or net worth. Splitting cover across two insurers can reduce concentration and speed up underwriting.

Directors and professionals

Directors' and officers' liability, and professional indemnity for doctors, lawyers and consultants, protect personal wealth from claims made against you at work.

NRIs buying in India

NRIs can usually buy Indian term and health insurance, often at lower premiums than abroad. Check residence and travel conditions, medical test options and how claims will be paid.

Should I surrender my old LIC, endowment or ULIP policy?

Not automatically. First buy the term cover you need, then decide whether to surrender, make the policy paid-up, or continue. The answer depends on how many years remain, the surrender value, the returns still to come, the tax on exit and your health today, since cover may be harder to buy later.

  1. 1

    List every policy

    Premium, cover, start and maturity dates, nominee and current surrender value, for the whole family.

  2. 2

    Work out the return still to come

    Compare the remaining premiums with the expected maturity value, not what has already been paid.

  3. 3

    Replace the cover first

    Never exit a policy until new term cover is issued, so your family is never unprotected.

  4. 4

    Choose surrender, paid-up or continue

    Paid-up keeps a reduced benefit without further premiums; surrender releases cash to invest now. IRDAI's 2024 rules improved surrender values on new policies.

How is insurance taxed in India?

Tax should not drive insurance decisions, but it affects the cost. Premium deductions are available only under the old tax regime, while death benefits are tax-free under both. Since 22 September 2025, individual life and health policies also carry no GST.

How do you make sure a claim gets paid?

Most rejected claims trace back to the proposal form, not the illness. Disclose every medical condition, habit and existing policy honestly, keep nominees current, and make sure your family knows where the policies are. Choose insurers with a record of settling claims quickly and in full.

  • Full disclosure

    Declare conditions, smoking, alcohol, hazardous hobbies and other policies. After five years of continuous health cover, insurers cannot reject a claim for non-disclosure except for fraud.

  • Nominees and records

    Nominees on every policy, consistent with your will, and one place where your family can find policy documents and contacts.

  • Cashless where possible

    Insurers must decide on cashless requests within an hour and on final discharge within three hours of the hospital's request.

  • Help when it matters

    Fintoo helps your family with documents, follow-ups and escalation, including to the insurer's grievance cell and the Insurance Ombudsman.

What changed in insurance rules in 2024 and 2025?

A run of changes from IRDAI and the GST Council made individual cover cheaper and fairer to policyholders. If you bought your policies before them, it is worth checking whether your cover and costs still make sense.

  • No GST on individual life and health policies

    From 22 September 2025, premiums on individual policies are exempt from GST, which was 18%. Group policies are still taxed.

  • No upper age limit for health insurance

    Insurers can no longer refuse cover to new buyers simply because of their age.

  • Shorter waiting and moratorium periods

    Waiting periods for pre-existing conditions are capped at three years, and the moratorium after which a claim cannot be contested for non-disclosure is now five years.

  • Faster cashless claims

    Cashless authorisation within one hour, and discharge approval within three hours.

  • 30-day free look and better surrender values

    You now have 30 days to return a new policy for a refund, and life policies sold from October 2024 pay higher surrender values if you exit early.

Insurance planning FAQs

What is insurance planning?

Insurance planning is identifying the financial risks your family cannot absorb, such as death, serious illness, disability, property loss or liability, and deciding which to insure, for how much and with which type of policy. It is part of your financial plan and is reviewed when your income, loans, family or assets change.

What are the benefits of having insurance?

Insurance transfers large, unpredictable losses to an insurer for a known premium. It lets your family keep its home, lifestyle and goals if you die or fall ill, stops a hospital bill from forcing you to sell investments at a bad time, and protects your assets from damage and legal claims.

How much life insurance cover do I need?

Add the present value of your family's future expenses, your outstanding loans and the goals you want funded, then subtract the investments your family could use and the cover you already have. A common shortcut is 10 to 15 times annual income, but for HNIs a needs-based calculation is more accurate.

Which kinds of insurance do I need?

Almost every family with dependants needs term life insurance and its own health insurance with a super top-up. Critical illness, personal accident and home insurance close the next biggest gaps. Business owners, directors and professionals should also consider key-person, buy-sell and liability cover.

Is term insurance better than endowment or ULIP plans?

For protection, yes. Term insurance gives the most cover for the premium, costs are transparent and you can stop at any time. Endowment and money-back plans combine low cover with modest returns, and ULIPs carry several layers of charges and a five-year lock-in. Buying term cover and investing separately is usually better.

How much health insurance is enough for a family in a metro city?

Enough to pay for a major illness at the hospital you would choose. Many metro families use a base family floater of ₹25 lakh or more with a super top-up of ₹50 lakh to ₹1 crore. Hold it in your own name, since employer cover ends when you change jobs or retire.

What is a super top-up health policy?

A super top-up pays hospital bills above a deductible you choose, such as ₹25 lakh, once the total of all bills in the policy year crosses it. Paired with a base policy of the same amount, it gives high cover at a much lower premium than a single large policy.

What is an MWP Act insurance policy?

It is a life policy taken by a married man under Section 6 of the Married Women's Property Act, 1874, naming his wife and children as beneficiaries. The payout is held in trust for them, so creditors, lenders and other relatives cannot claim it. It is useful for business owners and anyone with loans or personal guarantees.

Should I surrender my old LIC or endowment policy?

Not before buying the term cover you need. Then compare the premiums still to pay with the expected maturity value, check the surrender value and tax, and consider making the policy paid-up instead. Continuing can make sense if the policy is close to maturity; exiting often makes sense when many years remain.

Is life insurance maturity taxable in India?

Death benefits are always tax-free. Maturity proceeds of traditional policies issued from 1 April 2023 are taxable if total annual premiums on such policies exceed ₹5 lakh, and for ULIPs issued from 1 February 2021 if premiums exceed ₹2.5 lakh. Premiums above 10% of the sum assured also make maturity taxable.

Is there GST on insurance premiums?

Not on individual life and health insurance. From 22 September 2025, premiums on individual policies, which previously carried 18% GST, are exempt. Group policies, including employer-sponsored health and group term cover, still attract 18% GST.

Can I buy health insurance after 65?

Yes. IRDAI's 2024 rules removed the upper age limit for buying health insurance, so insurers must offer suitable products to older buyers. Premiums are higher, and pre-existing conditions can carry a waiting period of up to three years, so it is better to buy early and keep renewing.

Is it better to buy insurance online or through an advisor?

Buying online can be cheaper and quick, but an advisor helps you size cover correctly, compare exclusions and claim records, disclose health details properly, structure policies such as MWP Act plans, and support your family at claim time. The right choice depends on how complex your situation is.

How do I choose the right insurance policy?

Start from your needs, not the product. Decide the cover amount first, then compare policies on exclusions, waiting periods, room-rent limits, co-payment, claim settlement record and service. For life cover, check the insurer's claims record for large policies. Read the policy wording before you buy; you have 30 days to return it.

Why do insurance claims get rejected?

Most often because of non-disclosure of a medical condition, habit or existing policy in the proposal form, or because the treatment falls within a waiting period or exclusion. Honest disclosure, current nominees and understanding the policy's exclusions prevent most rejections.
