NRI taxation, explained. Then handled end to end.

Understand your residential status, what India taxes, how DTAA relief works and how to sell property or move money abroad without surprises. When you are ready, Fintoo's chartered accountants take care of the filing, certificates and paperwork.

In short

A non-resident Indian pays Indian tax only on income earned or received in India, such as rent, NRO interest and capital gains. Salary and investments abroad are not taxed in India. Interest on NRE and FCNR deposits is tax-free, and a tax treaty (DTAA) can stop the same income being taxed twice.

Who is an NRI for tax, and how is residential status decided?

For income tax, your status depends on the number of days you spend in India in a financial year, not your passport or visa. Most Indians working abroad are non-residents if they spend fewer than 182 days in India, though high Indian income and frequent visits can change that.

Status is worked out afresh every year, from 1 April to 31 March. It decides whether India taxes only your Indian income or your worldwide income, so it is the first thing to settle before filing.

NRI

Indian income only

RNOR

Mostly Indian income

Resident

Worldwide income

Find your status in three questions

  1. 1
    Were you in India for 182 days or more this financial year?

    Yes You are a resident.   No Go to question 2.

  2. 2
    Were you in India 60 days or more this year, and 365 days or more over the previous four years?

    If you are an Indian citizen or of Indian origin visiting India, read 60 as 182 days, or 120 days if your Indian income is above ₹15 lakh. Indian crew and those leaving India for a job also use 182. If yes, you are resident, possibly RNOR.

  3. 3
    Is your Indian income above ₹15 lakh, and are you not taxed in any other country?

    Yes An Indian citizen is deemed resident (as RNOR).  No You are a non-resident.

Indian income here excludes income from foreign sources. Days of arrival and departure usually both count. Edge cases need a proper review.

Which income is taxable for an NRI in India?

India taxes an NRI only on income that is earned in India or received in India. Foreign salary, foreign rent and investments held abroad are outside the Indian tax net. Most Indian income has tax deducted at source, often at higher rates than a resident pays.

What is the income tax rate for NRIs in India?

NRIs pay the same slab rates as residents on income like rent and interest, and the new regime is the default. But NRIs cannot claim the rebate that makes income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free for residents, so tax starts above the ₹4 lakh basic exemption.

  • Capital gains are taxed at their own special rates, not slabs.
  • The old regime is still available if your deductions make it cheaper.
  • Section 80C-type deductions apply only in the old regime.

How does the DTAA stop you paying tax twice?

India has tax treaties with around 90 countries. A treaty either lets only one country tax an income, or lets you claim credit in one country for tax paid in the other. You can use whichever is better: the treaty rate or Indian law.

To claim treaty benefits in India, you need a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) from your country of residence and Form 10F filed online.

Example: ₹1,00,000 of NRO interest

Without treaty: TDS at 30% plus cess₹31,200
With treaty rate of 15% and a TRC₹15,000

Tax held back drops by ₹16,200. Treaty rates differ by country and income type.

  • Exemption methodIncome taxed in only one of the two countries.
  • Credit methodTaxed in both, with credit for tax already paid.

How is property bought or sold by an NRI taxed?

NRIs can buy homes and commercial property in India, but not farmland, plantations or farmhouses unless inherited. When an NRI sells, the buyer must deduct TDS, often on the full sale price, which can lock up far more than the actual tax. A lower TDS certificate fixes that.

  1. 1

    Work out the gain

    Held over 24 months, the gain is long term and taxed at 12.5%, without indexation for NRIs.

  2. 2

    Apply for lower TDS

    A lower or nil TDS certificate lets the buyer deduct tax on the gain, not the whole price.

  3. 3

    Buyer deducts and deposits

    Budget 2026 lets resident buyers deposit this TDS using their PAN, with no separate TAN.

  4. 4

    File and repatriate

    Claim any excess TDS as a refund in your return, then move the money abroad.

Save tax on the gain

NRIs can reinvest long-term gains in another Indian home, or up to ₹50 lakh in 54EC bonds within six months, to save capital gains tax.

Rent from the property

Tenants must deduct TDS on rent paid to an NRI. Rent should go to an NRO account, and a 30% standard deduction applies.

Inherited property

Inheriting is not taxed. On sale, the original owner's cost and holding period carry over to you.

How can NRIs repatriate money from India?

Money in an NRE or FCNR account can be moved abroad freely. From an NRO account, you can repatriate up to US$1 million each financial year, including property sale proceeds, after taxes are paid. From 1 April 2026, the paperwork uses Forms 145 and 146, which replace Forms 15CA and 15CB.

Are gifts to or from NRIs taxed?

Gifts from relatives are not taxed, whichever country they come from. Gifts above ₹50,000 a year from non-relatives are taxed as income in the receiver's hands, and since 2019 a gift from a resident to an NRI counts as Indian income. Foreign exchange rules apply separately.

Moving back to India? What changes for your taxes?

Returning NRIs often qualify as Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) for up to two or three years. As an RNOR, your foreign income is still not taxed in India, which gives you time to restructure foreign assets.

You are usually RNOR if you were a non-resident in 9 of the previous 10 years, or spent 729 days or fewer in India over the previous 7 years.

  1. 1

    Before you land

    Plan the timing of foreign asset sales and bonuses while still non-resident.

  2. 2

    Convert your accounts

    Redesignate NRE and NRO accounts to resident accounts. Move foreign currency to an RFC account if you wish.

  3. 3

    Use the RNOR years

    Foreign income stays outside Indian tax. Restructure investments before you become fully resident.

  4. 4

    Become a full resident

    Worldwide income is taxed, and every foreign asset must be reported in Schedule FA.

Do NRIs need to file an income tax return in India?

An NRI must file if Indian income is above the basic exemption of ₹4 lakh, and should file whenever extra TDS was deducted, because a return is the only way to get it refunded. Filing is fully online and does not need a visit to India.

Usual due date

31 July

For NRIs without a tax audit, after the end of the tax year. Filing late attracts a fee and interest, and you may lose the right to carry losses forward.

Common NRI tax mistakes, and what to do instead

Most NRIs overpay not because of high rates, but because of paperwork that was missed. These are the ones we fix most often.

  • 01

    Keeping resident bank accounts after moving abroad

    Instead: tell your bank and convert accounts to NRO or NRE. Resident accounts are not allowed for NRIs.

  • 02

    Not filing because TDS was already deducted

    Instead: file anyway. TDS is often far more than the actual tax, and only a return gets it back.

  • 03

    Selling property without a lower TDS certificate

    Instead: apply before the sale so the buyer deducts tax on your gain, not the full price.

  • 04

    Ignoring DTAA relief

    Instead: get a TRC and file Form 10F to use lower treaty rates on interest and dividends.

  • 05

    Counting days loosely

    Instead: track days in India from your passport. A few extra days can change your status.

  • 06

    Investing NRO money in NRE deposits

    Instead: keep Indian income in NRO accounts; mixing them breaks repatriation and tax rules.

NRI taxation FAQs

Clear answers to what NRIs ask most about Indian tax.

Last reviewed 1 October 2026 by Mihir Shah, AVP, Financial Planning, Fintoo.

How is residential status determined for income tax?

You are resident in a financial year if you spend 182 days or more in India, or 60 days or more in the year plus 365 days over the previous four years. For Indian citizens and persons of Indian origin visiting India, the 60 days becomes 182, or 120 if Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh. Anyone else is a non-resident. An Indian citizen with Indian income above ₹15 lakh who is not taxed in any other country is deemed resident.

What is the tax rate for NRIs in India?

NRIs pay the same slab rates as residents on income such as rent and interest. Under the default new regime for Tax Year 2026-27, income up to ₹4 lakh is tax-free and rates rise from 5% to 30% above ₹24 lakh. NRIs cannot claim the rebate that makes income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free for residents. Capital gains are taxed at special rates, and surcharge and 4% cess apply.

Is income earned abroad taxable in India for NRIs?

No. A non-resident is taxed in India only on income earned or received in India, such as rent, interest on NRO accounts, dividends and capital gains on Indian assets. Salary for work done abroad and income from foreign investments are not taxed in India, even if later transferred to an Indian account.

Is interest on NRE and FCNR accounts taxable?

Interest on NRE accounts and FCNR deposits is tax-free in India while you are a non-resident, and no TDS is deducted. Interest on NRO accounts is taxable, with TDS at 30% plus surcharge and cess, which can be lowered to the tax treaty rate if you provide a Tax Residency Certificate. NRE and FCNR interest becomes taxable once you are resident.

How does the DTAA help NRIs reduce their tax liability?

A Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement between India and your country of residence either lets only one country tax a particular income, or lets you claim credit for tax paid in one country against tax in the other. It can also lower TDS on Indian interest and dividends. To claim it, you need a Tax Residency Certificate and Form 10F filed online.

Can an NRI avail of tax benefits under the Indian Income Tax Act?

Yes. NRIs can claim deductions such as life insurance premiums, tuition fees, home loan principal and interest, NPS contributions, health insurance and donations, but most of these are available only under the old tax regime. Some benefits meant for residents, such as opening a new PPF account and the senior citizen interest deduction, are not available to NRIs.

Do NRIs need to file an income tax return in India?

An NRI must file a return if Indian income exceeds the basic exemption limit of ₹4 lakh. It is also worth filing whenever TDS was deducted on rent, interest or a property sale, because a return is the only way to claim a refund of excess tax. The usual due date for NRIs without a tax audit is 31 July after the tax year ends.

Can an NRI claim a tax refund in India?

Yes. When the TDS deducted is more than the actual tax due, an NRI can claim the difference as a refund by filing an income tax return. The refund is paid to a pre-validated Indian bank account, such as an NRO or NRE account, usually within a few weeks of the return being processed.

What TDS applies when an NRI sells property in India?

The buyer must deduct TDS before paying an NRI seller. For property held over 24 months, it is based on 12.5% long-term capital gains tax plus surcharge and cess, and is often applied to the full sale price. The NRI can apply for a lower or nil TDS certificate so tax is deducted only on the actual gain. Budget 2026 lets resident buyers deposit this TDS using their PAN instead of a separate TAN.

How much money can an NRI repatriate from India?

Balances in NRE and FCNR accounts can be repatriated freely. From an NRO account, an NRI can repatriate up to US$1 million per financial year, including sale proceeds of property, after paying applicable taxes. The bank needs Form 145 and, for taxable remittances above ₹5 lakh, a chartered accountant's certificate in Form 146.

What are Forms 145 and 146?

From 1 April 2026, under the Income-tax Act, 2025, Form 145 and Form 146 replace Forms 15CA and 15CB. Form 145 is the online declaration of a remittance abroad, and Form 146 is the chartered accountant's certificate confirming the tax position, needed for taxable remittances above ₹5 lakh.

Are gifts to NRIs taxable in India?

Gifts from relatives are not taxable, however large. Gifts from non-relatives are taxable in the receiver's hands if they total more than ₹50,000 in a year, and a gift from a resident to an NRI is treated as income arising in India. Gifts on marriage and by will or inheritance are exempt.

What is RNOR status and who qualifies?

Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) is a transitional status, usually for returning NRIs. You are RNOR if you were a non-resident in 9 of the previous 10 years, or spent 729 days or fewer in India over the previous 7 years. RNORs are taxed in India on Indian income, but generally not on foreign income.

Can NRIs buy agricultural land in India?

No. Under FEMA, NRIs and OCIs cannot buy agricultural land, plantation property or farmhouses in India. They can buy residential and commercial property, and they can hold agricultural land that they inherit.
