What is an income tax notice, and is yours genuine?

It is an official communication from the Income Tax Department about your return or tax. Every genuine notice carries a Document Identification Number (DIN) and appears in your account on the income tax e-filing portal. You can check any notice there before you act on it.

Getting a notice is stressful, especially the first time. But a notice is not an accusation. Very often it is an automated check, and a clear, documented reply closes it.

Check a notice in four steps

  1. 01

    Find the DIN

    Printed on every genuine notice.

  2. 02

    Authenticate it

    Use "Authenticate notice" on the e-filing portal.

  3. 03

    Note section and year

    They tell you what is asked and for when.

  4. 04

    Mark the deadline

    Reply before it, or ask for more time.

Six terms worth knowing

DIN
Document Identification Number: the unique ID that proves a notice is genuine.
AIS and Form 26AS
The department's record of your income, TDS and big transactions.
Intimation
The result of processing your return: accepted, refund due or tax due.
e-Proceedings
The portal section where you view notices and file replies online.
Assessment year
The year after the income was earned. Under the new Act, this is called the tax year.
Rectification
A request to correct an obvious mistake in an order or intimation.

Why do taxpayers receive income tax notices?

The department matches your return against data from employers, banks, brokers and registrars. Most notices are caused by a gap between what you reported and what the department already knows. These are the most common triggers.

  • Income left out

    Interest, rent, capital gains or freelance income missing from the return.

  • TDS mismatch

    TDS claimed does not match Form 26AS or AIS.

  • High-value transactions

    Large cash deposits, property deals or investments not explained.

  • Wrong deductions

    Claims without proof, or claims not allowed in the chosen regime.

  • Defects in the return

    Wrong ITR form, missing schedules or calculation errors.

  • Late or missing returns

    Not filing despite taxable income, or tax paid short.

!What happens if you ignore a notice

  • The proposal becomes finalThe department's figures are used without your side.
  • Interest keeps adding upOn any tax demand, every month it stays unpaid.
  • PenaltiesFor not complying, and up to 200% of tax for misreporting.
  • Refunds held backFuture refunds can be adjusted against the demand.

The fix for all of these: reply on time, with documents, even if you agree with the department.

What are the common types of income tax notices?

Each notice is identified by its section, which tells you what the department wants. Some are routine and need only a check; others start a detailed examination and need careful handling. The section numbers below are those of the Income-tax Act, 1961, used for years up to 2025-26.

What about the new Income-tax Act, 2025?

From 1 April 2026, new proceedings fall under the Income-tax Act, 2025, which renumbers these provisions. Notices for earlier years still cite the 1961 Act. The reasons behind notices and the way to respond stay largely the same. We read every notice to confirm which law and section apply.

How do you respond to an income tax notice?

Almost every notice is now answered online, through the e-Proceedings section of the e-filing portal. A good reply is short, factual and backed by documents, and it answers exactly what was asked.

  1. 1

    Verify the notice

    Check the DIN on the portal. Never pay or share details through links in emails or texts.

  2. 2

    Find the real reason

    Compare your return with AIS, Form 26AS and bank statements to see what does not match.

  3. 3

    Gather your proof

    Form 16, broker statements, rent receipts, investment proofs and sale deeds, as relevant.

  4. 4

    Choose the right fix

    Reply with an explanation, file a revised or updated return, request rectification, or pay.

  5. 5

    Submit on e-Proceedings

    Upload the reply and documents before the deadline, or ask for an adjournment in time.

  6. 6

    Keep the trail

    Save the acknowledgement and follow up until the order or closure arrives.

The clock starts when the notice is served

How long do you have to reply?

Deadlines run from 15 days to about two months, depending on the notice. For inquiries and scrutiny, the date is set in the notice itself. If you need more time, ask for it before the deadline, with a reason.

  1. Defective return139(9) 15 days
  2. Refund adjustment245 30 days
  3. Tax demand156 30 days
  4. Appeal to CIT(A)after an order 30 days
  5. Appeal to ITATafter CIT(A) order ~2 months

Typical windows under the 1961 Act. Always go by the date printed on your notice.

What if you disagree with the order? Appeals and ITAT

If the department passes an order you believe is wrong, you can correct obvious mistakes through rectification, or appeal on the merits. Most disputes are settled at the first or second level. Fintoo's team also files and argues appeals before the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT).

  1. Step 1

    Rectification

    For clear errors in an intimation or order, like a missed TDS credit.

  2. Step 2

    Commissioner (Appeals)

    The first appeal, usually within 30 days of the order, filed online.

  3. Step 3

    ITAT

    The tribunal on facts and law, within about two months of the CIT(A) order.

  4. Step 4

    High Court

    Only on a substantial question of law.

How can you avoid getting an income tax notice?

Most notices are preventable. Check your AIS before filing, report every income, and keep proof for every claim. These habits close the usual gaps.

  • 01

    Filing without checking AIS and Form 26AS

    Instead: match every income and TDS entry before you submit, and give feedback on wrong AIS entries.

  • 02

    Leaving out small incomes

    Instead: report savings interest, FD interest, dividends and gains, even if they seem small.

  • 03

    Claiming deductions without proof

    Instead: keep receipts, and check the deduction is allowed in the regime you chose.

  • 04

    Big cash transactions

    Instead: use banking channels, and be ready to explain the source of large deposits or purchases.

  • 05

    Choosing the wrong ITR form

    Instead: pick the form that fits all your income, such as ITR-2 for capital gains.

  • 06

    Ignoring e-campaign emails

    Instead: respond to compliance messages; they are early warnings, not notices yet.

Income tax notice FAQs

Clear answers to what taxpayers ask when a notice arrives.

Last reviewed 1 October 2026 by Mihir Shah, AVP, Financial Planning, Fintoo.

Why do taxpayers receive income tax notices?

Taxpayers receive income tax notices for reasons such as a mismatch between the return and the department's records in AIS or Form 26AS, income left out of the return, unexplained high-value transactions, unsupported deductions, errors in the return, or non-payment or short payment of tax. Many notices are automated checks rather than accusations.

How can I check whether an income tax notice is genuine?

Every genuine notice carries a Document Identification Number (DIN). Enter it in the 'Authenticate notice/order issued by ITD' option on the income tax e-filing portal, and log in to see the notice under e-Proceedings. Never pay or share details through links in emails or text messages.

What is an intimation under section 143(1)?

It is the result of the department processing your return. It shows whether your return was accepted as filed, whether a refund is due, or whether there is a demand because of a mismatch, such as a TDS credit that was not matched. If you disagree with a demand, you can file a rectification request or respond online.

What is a defective return notice under section 139(9)?

It means your return has an error, such as the wrong ITR form, missing schedules or tax not fully paid. You usually get 15 days to correct the defect and refile. If you do not, the return can be treated as if it was never filed.

What is the difference between section 142(1) and 143(2) notices?

A section 142(1) notice asks for specific documents or information, or asks you to file a return. A section 143(2) notice means your return has been selected for scrutiny, a detailed assessment where you must support your income, deductions and transactions. Both need a timely, documented reply.

What is a section 148 notice?

A section 148 notice is issued when the department believes some income escaped assessment in an earlier year. It is usually preceded by a show-cause notice under section 148A, where you can explain why the case should not be reopened. Cases can generally be reopened up to about three years later, or up to five years where the escaped income is ₹50 lakh or more.

What does a section 245 notice mean?

It means the department proposes to adjust a refund due to you against an outstanding demand from another year. You can agree, partly agree or disagree, with reasons, usually within 30 days. If you do not respond, the adjustment is made.

How can taxpayers avoid receiving income tax notices?

Check your Annual Information Statement and Form 26AS before filing, report every income including interest and capital gains, claim only deductions you can prove, choose the correct ITR form, avoid large unexplained cash transactions, file and pay on time, and respond to compliance messages from the department.

What are the consequences of ignoring an income tax notice?

If you ignore a notice, the department can finalise your assessment based on its own information, raise a demand with interest, levy penalties for non-compliance or for under-reporting and misreporting income, and adjust future refunds against the demand. In serious cases, prosecution is possible.

Can a taxpayer request more time to respond to an income tax notice?

Yes. You can request an adjournment through the e-Proceedings section of the e-filing portal before the deadline, giving a reason, such as needing time to collect documents. Granting extra time is at the officer's discretion, so request it early and do not assume it will be allowed.

How do I respond to an income tax notice online?

Log in to the income tax e-filing portal, open Pending Actions and then e-Proceedings, select the notice, and submit your response with supporting documents. Depending on the notice, you may also need to file a revised or updated return, a rectification request or pay the demand.

What should I do if I disagree with a tax demand?

First check whether the demand comes from an obvious error, such as a missed TDS credit, and file a rectification request if so. If you disagree on the merits, respond with evidence or file an appeal with the Commissioner of Income Tax (Appeals), usually within 30 days of the order. You can request a stay on recovery while the appeal is pending.

What is the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT)?

The ITAT is the second level of appeal in income tax matters. If you do not agree with an order of the Commissioner (Appeals), you can appeal to the ITAT, which decides questions of both fact and law. Fintoo's team prepares and argues appeals before the ITAT.

Do section numbers change under the Income-tax Act, 2025?

Yes. From 1 April 2026, new proceedings fall under the Income-tax Act, 2025, which renumbers most provisions. Notices for earlier years continue to refer to sections of the Income-tax Act, 1961, such as 143(1) and 148. The reasons for notices and the way to respond remain largely similar.
