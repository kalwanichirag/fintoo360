Take your portfolio from national to international.

Learn how Indians invest in US and global stocks, which route suits you, what the rupee does to your returns, and how foreign shares are taxed in India. When you are ready, Fintoo's global advisors help you decide how much to hold abroad and how to get there.

In short

International equity means owning shares of companies listed outside India, most often in the US. Resident Indians can invest up to US$250,000 a year under the RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme, directly or through mutual funds. It spreads your risk beyond one economy and one currency, but adds currency swings, extra tax paperwork and, for direct US holdings, US estate tax rules.

What is international equity, and how do Indians invest abroad?

International equity is any share or equity fund whose companies are listed outside India. You can buy them directly through an overseas broker account funded under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, or indirectly through Indian mutual funds and ETFs that invest abroad. Both routes are legal, regulated and open to resident individuals.

India is roughly 3% to 4% of the world's listed market value. Companies that make most of the world's smartphones, chips, cloud software, medicines and payment networks are listed elsewhere. Owning a slice of them is what this page is about.

Six terms worth knowing

LRS
The RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme: up to US$250,000 per person each financial year.
ETF
An exchange-traded fund: one share that holds a whole index, such as the S&P 500.
Fractional share
Part of one share, so you invest an amount rather than a number of shares.
W-8BEN
A US form confirming you are not a US person, so India's tax treaty rate applies.
TCS
Tax collected at source by your bank on large remittances. It is credited back to you.
Schedule FA
The part of your Indian tax return where every foreign asset must be reported.

Why invest outside India, and what are the risks?

Why restrict your global dreams to local investments? A global slice lets you own businesses India does not list and spreads your wealth across economies and currencies. It also brings risks that Indian shares do not have. Here is both sides, at a glance.

+Why people invest globally

  • Opportunities across the globe

    Own global leaders in chips, cloud, AI, biotech and payments.

  • Less dependence on one economy

    Global and Indian markets do not always rise and fall together.

  • A dollar cushion

    If the rupee weakens, your dollar assets are worth more in rupees.

  • Start small, grow big

    Fractional shares let you start with a few thousand rupees.

  • Dollar goals, dollar savings

    Study, travel or a home abroad are priced in foreign currency.

  • Low-cost index ETFs

    Broad US ETFs can cost well under 0.1% a year.

!Risks to keep in mind

  • Currency cuts both waysA stronger rupee reduces your returns in rupees.
  • US estate taxDirect US holdings above US$60,000 can be taxed at death.
  • More tax paperworkSchedule FA, Form 67 and foreign tax credit every year.
  • Crowded in a few giantsA handful of US tech companies dominate the main indices.

The fix for most of these: size the global slice to your goals, pick the right route, and keep the paperwork clean.

What are the ways to invest in US and global stocks from India?

There are four main routes. Direct investing gives you the widest choice; Indian mutual funds and ETFs are the simplest; GIFT City sits in between. They differ in minimums, tax, paperwork and whether US estate tax can apply.

Why do some international funds stop taking money?

SEBI caps the total that all Indian mutual funds can invest abroad at US$7 billion, with a smaller cap per fund house. When a fund house nears its limit, it pauses lump sums or SIPs, sometimes for months. Direct investing under LRS is not affected by this cap.

Already hold foreign ESOPs or RSUs?

Shares from a foreign employer count as international equity too, and they must go in Schedule FA. They are often a large bet on one company. Asset migration, selling part of the vested shares and spreading the money across broader holdings, can cut that risk. Time the sales around tax.

How do fractional shares work?

Most US brokers let you buy part of a share. You decide how much money to invest, and the broker gives you the matching fraction, so even companies priced at hundreds of dollars a share are within reach.

Fractional units earn dividends and rise or fall in value in proportion. They make it possible to build a spread-out global portfolio with small amounts, though some brokers do not let you move fractional units to another broker, and some stocks may not be offered in fractions.

Which goals is international equity suited for?

Global shares are a long-term growth asset, like Indian equity. They fit best when a goal is five or more years away, or when the goal itself is priced in a foreign currency. For most Indian investors, a global slice of 10% to 30% of equity is a common starting range, not a rule.

Ask a global advisor

How are US and foreign shares taxed in India?

As a resident Indian, you pay Indian tax on your worldwide income. Foreign shares held for more than 24 months are taxed at 12.5% on the gain; sold sooner, the gain is added to your income. Dividends are taxed in both countries, but the US tax is credited against your Indian tax.

Report every foreign asset in Schedule FA

Residents must list each foreign account and holding, including ESOPs, RSUs and cash, in Schedule FA, for the calendar year ending 31 December. Foreign income goes in Schedule FSI. Missing this can attract heavy penalties under the Black Money Act, even when no tax is due.

Gains are worked out in rupees

Your cost and sale price are converted to rupees at the prescribed exchange rates, so a gain can come partly from the rupee weakening. A loss in dollars can even be a gain in rupees, and the other way round.

Rates exclude surcharge and 4% cess. See our tax planning guide and NRI taxation services.

What happens to your US shares if you die?

India has no inheritance tax, but the US does. For non-US investors, US-based assets above US$60,000 can attract US estate tax at rates of up to 40%, and India and the US have no estate tax treaty to soften it. This is the risk most Indian investors in US stocks have never heard of.

  • In India: your nominee or heir receives the shares without tax. They take on your original cost and holding period, and pay capital gains tax only when they sell.
  • In the US: the broker usually needs a death certificate, transfer documents and, above the threshold, clearance from the US tax authority before releasing the shares.
  • Ways to plan: spread holdings across family members, use Indian international funds or ETFs domiciled outside the US, keep a nominee and a will, and review as the value grows.

Are you an NRI?

LRS applies only to Indian residents. NRIs usually invest abroad through a broker in their country of residence, and some global platforms accept NRIs depending on where they live. Tax then depends on your residency. Our advisors can tell you what applies in your case.

Common mistakes when investing abroad, and what to do instead

Most problems with global investing are not about picking the wrong stock, but about size, tax and paperwork. These are the ones we see most often.

  • 01

    Buying only famous tech names

    Instead: start with a broad index ETF, then add individual stocks as a small satellite.

  • 02

    Forgetting Schedule FA

    Instead: report every foreign holding each year, even a small one with no income.

  • 03

    Paying dividend tax twice

    Instead: file Form 67 before your return to claim credit for US tax withheld.

  • 04

    Ignoring US estate tax

    Instead: once direct US holdings near US$60,000, review the route and the ownership.

  • 05

    Many small remittances

    Instead: fewer, planned transfers cut bank charges and currency mark-ups.

  • 06

    Going global with no plan

    Instead: decide your global share of equity first, then rebalance to it once a year.

International investing FAQs

Clear answers to what Indian investors ask before going global.

Can Indians legally invest in US stocks?

Yes. Resident Indians can invest in foreign shares and ETFs under the RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS), through an overseas broker account. You can also invest indirectly through Indian mutual funds and ETFs that hold foreign shares, which does not use your LRS limit.

How much can I invest in US stocks from India?

Each resident individual can remit up to US$250,000 per financial year under LRS, across all purposes combined, including travel, education and gifts. Family members each have their own limit. Tax collected at source (TCS) of 20% applies to investment remittances above ₹10 lakh a year, and is adjustable against your tax.

How do fractional shares work?

Fractional shares let you invest an amount of money instead of buying whole shares. If a stock is priced at $158 and you invest $100, you own about 0.63 of a share. Fractions rise and fall in value and earn dividends in proportion, so you can build a diversified global portfolio with small amounts.

What is the minimum amount to start investing in US stocks?

With fractional shares, many platforms let you start with as little as one US dollar, though bank remittance charges make very small transfers costly. Through Indian international mutual funds, you can start an SIP from a few hundred rupees a month, when the fund is accepting money.

Do I need to pay tax on foreign dividends in both the US and India? Can I claim credit?

Yes, US dividends are taxed in both countries. The US withholds 25% for Indian residents who have filed a W-8BEN (30% without it). In India, the dividend is taxed at your slab rate, and you can claim the US tax as a credit under the India-US tax treaty by filing Form 67 before your income tax return.

How are capital gains on US stocks taxed in India?

Foreign shares held for more than 24 months are long-term and taxed at 12.5% without indexation. Shares sold within 24 months are short-term and taxed at your slab rate. Gains are calculated in rupees, so currency moves affect them. The US generally does not tax capital gains of non-resident investors.

Am I liable to pay tax when I remit the funds back to India?

No. Bringing your money back to India is not itself taxed. You owe Indian tax on capital gains and dividends in the year you earn them, whether the money stays abroad or comes home. Inward remittances also do not attract TCS.

What are the tax implications in India if my shares pass to my nominee after my death?

India has no inheritance tax, so your nominee or legal heir receives the shares tax-free. They inherit your original cost and holding period, and pay capital gains tax only when they sell. Separately, US estate tax may apply if the US assets you hold directly exceed US$60,000, at rates of up to 40%.

What is US estate tax and does it apply to Indians?

US estate tax is charged on the value of US-situs assets, such as shares of US companies, when the owner dies. For non-US persons, only the first US$60,000 is exempt, and rates go up to 40%. India and the US have no estate tax treaty. Indian mutual funds that invest abroad avoid this exposure.

What insurance or protection do I get for my account?

If your US broker is a member of the Securities Investor Protection Corporation (SIPC), your account is protected up to US$500,000, including up to US$250,000 in cash, if the broker fails. SIPC does not protect against market losses. Your securities are held in your name with a regulated custodian.

Can NRIs invest in US stocks?

LRS is only for resident Indians. NRIs can usually invest in US stocks through a broker in their country of residence, and some global investing platforms accept NRIs depending on where they live. Eligibility, tax and reporting depend on your residency, so check with an advisor first.

What documents are required to open a US stock account?

You typically need your PAN, Aadhaar or passport as address proof, a bank statement or cancelled cheque, a photograph and signature, and an online W-8BEN form. Your bank will also ask for an LRS declaration, usually Form A2, when you send money abroad.

Do I need to report US stocks in my Indian tax return?

Yes. Resident taxpayers must report every foreign holding, including shares, ESOPs, RSUs and foreign bank balances, in Schedule FA, even if they earned no income from it. Foreign income goes in Schedule FSI. Failing to report can attract penalties under the Black Money Act.

Is it better to invest through international mutual funds or directly?

International mutual funds are simpler, avoid US estate tax and need no foreign reporting, but offer limited choice and can pause new investments when industry overseas limits are reached. Direct investing gives the widest choice and lower ongoing costs but adds paperwork. Many investors use both.

How does the rupee affect returns on US stocks?

Your rupee return is roughly the dollar return plus the change in the rupee-dollar rate. If the rupee weakens by 2% a year, an 8% dollar return becomes about 10% in rupees. If the rupee strengthens, your rupee return falls. Currency moves can work for or against you in any given year.
