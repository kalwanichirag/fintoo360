Work out exactly how much you need, invest it around your timeline, and draw it down tax-efficiently for 30 years or more. A SEBI-registered Fintoo advisor builds the plan with you and reviews it as life changes.

In short

Most Indian families need a corpus of about 25 to 30 times their annual expenses at retirement, invested in buckets by when the money is needed and withdrawn at around 3% to 3.5% a year. HNIs also need to plan tax on withdrawals, rising medical costs, business or property liquidity, and succession.

What is retirement planning, and why is it different for HNIs?

Retirement planning is working out how much money you will need after you stop working, then building and managing the investments that will pay for it. For HNIs and business owners the harder part is rarely saving enough. It is turning complex, concentrated wealth into reliable, tax-efficient income that lasts three decades.

  • Retirement can last 35 years

    Retire at 55 and live to 90, and your corpus has to fund more years than you spent building it, with no employer pension to fall back on.

  • Healthcare rises faster than prices

    Medical costs in India are rising about 11.5% to 14% a year, well above headline inflation, and premiums climb steeply after 60.

  • Wealth is often locked up

    A business, property, ESOPs or a single stock can make up most of an HNI's net worth. The plan has to create liquidity from them at the right time.

  • Tax decides what you keep

    Interest, rent, annuities and capital gains are taxed very differently. The order you draw on them can change your post-tax income meaningfully every year.

  • Your wealth has to outlive you, deliberately

    Wills, nominations, trusts and business succession decide how the corpus reaches your spouse and children. They belong in the same plan as your retirement income.

How much money do you need to retire in India?

A practical starting point is 25 to 30 times your annual expenses in the year you retire. First inflate today's spending to your retirement year, then multiply. At 6% inflation, spending doubles roughly every 12 years, which is why a comfortable lifestyle today needs a much larger number than most people expect.

Is ₹5 crore enough to retire in India?

It can be, but usually not for an HNI household. At a sustainable withdrawal rate of 3% to 3.5%, ₹5 crore supports about ₹1.25 lakh to ₹1.45 lakh a month in the first year of retirement, rising with inflation. That suits a modest lifestyle in most cities, not a metro lifestyle with travel, domestic staff and private healthcare, and not a retirement that starts before 55.

What is a safe withdrawal rate in India?

Many Indian planners use 3% to 3.5% a year rather than the 4% rule common in the US. Inflation in India is higher and early retirements are longer. A plan with rental income, a pension or flexible spending can safely draw more; one that is heavy in equity or starts at 50 should draw less.

The bucket strategy: income you can rely on, growth you still need

The bucket strategy divides your corpus by when you will need the money. You spend from a safe first bucket and refill it from the others in good years, so a market fall never forces you to sell equity at a loss.

Fintoo sizes each bucket from your actual spending, pension, rent and tax position, and reviews the refill every year.

  1. Bucket 1 · Years 0 to 3

    Income and safety

    Two to three years of expenses, where the value doesn't swing. Pays your monthly income.

    Typically holds

    • Liquid funds
    • Arbitrage funds
    • SCSS
    • Short FDs

  2. Bucket 2 · Years 3 to 10

    Stability

    The next five to seven years of spending, earning more than cash with limited volatility. Refills bucket 1.

    Typically holds

    • Government and high-grade bonds
    • Debt funds
    • Hybrid funds

  3. Bucket 3 · Year 10 onwards

    Long-term growth

    Money you won't touch for a decade, invested to beat inflation and fund the later years and your legacy.

    Typically holds

    • Equity mutual funds
    • Direct equity
    • PMS
    • International equity

What changed in 2025–26

New NPS exit rules: up to 80% as a lump sum

PFRDA's December 2025 amendments let non-government subscribers take up to 80% of their NPS corpus as a lump sum at normal exit, with at least 20% used for an annuity when the corpus is above ₹12 lakh. You can also stay invested until 85. Only 60% of the lump sum is currently tax-exempt, so the timing and size of the withdrawal needs planning.

How to withdraw your retirement income tax-efficiently

Retirement income is taxed by its source, not its size. Pension, annuity, FD and SCSS interest are taxed at your slab rate, while long-term equity gains are taxed at 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year. Drawing income in the right order can lower your tax every year of retirement.

From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act. Section numbers changed, but rates and senior-citizen benefits carried over unchanged.

  1. 1

    Use a systematic withdrawal plan (SWP) from equity funds

    Each SWP payment is mostly your own capital coming back, and only the gain portion is taxed. For long-held equity funds that gain is taxed at 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year, often far less than interest taxed at 30%.

  2. 2

    Harvest the ₹1.25 lakh equity exemption every year

    Book long-term equity gains up to the exemption each year, for you and your spouse separately, and reinvest. It resets your purchase cost and lowers future tax.

  3. 3

    Choose the right tax regime each year

    The new regime makes income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free after rebate; the old regime keeps senior-citizen deductions such as ₹50,000 on interest income and higher health-insurance deductions. The better choice can change as your income mix changes.

  4. 4

    Split income across family members

    Holding investments in both spouses' names, within clubbing rules, uses two sets of exemptions and lower slabs. For larger estates, trusts can also help.

Retirement planning for your situation

Every HNI retirement has one hard problem at its centre. These are the ones Fintoo advisors work on most.

Business owners and founders

Separate personal wealth from the business, plan what happens to income if you step back, and structure the proceeds of a sale or stake dilution into a diversified corpus, including capital gains tax.

Senior professionals with ESOPs

Reduce concentration in your employer's stock on a schedule, time sales for tax, and turn vesting into retirement savings rather than lifestyle spending.

Early retirement at 45 to 55

A 35- to 45-year retirement needs a lower withdrawal rate, more equity for longer, and a health cover plan before you lose employer insurance.

Property-heavy families

Decide which properties to keep for rent, which to sell and when, and how to reinvest the proceeds so the family isn't asset-rich and cash-poor.

NRIs returning to India

Plan residential status and RNOR years, repatriation, tax on foreign pensions and retirement accounts, and which assets to hold in which country and currency.

Already retired

Rebuild a scattered portfolio into buckets, cut idle cash and costly policies, lower tax on income, and put wills, nominations and trusts in place.

Common retirement planning mistakes

  • Planning with today's expenses instead of inflated ones
  • Moving everything to FDs at retirement and losing to inflation
  • Relying on employer health cover that ends when you retire
  • Counting the family home or business as retirement income
  • Buying insurance-linked "pension plans" with high hidden costs
  • Leaving wills and nominations out of the retirement plan

Retirement planning FAQs

Straight answers to the questions HNIs ask us most.

What is retirement planning?

Retirement planning is the process of working out how much money you will need to live the way you want after you stop working, then building and managing the investments that will produce that income. For HNIs it also covers tax-efficient withdrawals, healthcare costs, liquidity from a business or property, and passing wealth on.

When should I start planning for retirement?

As early as possible, ideally 15 to 20 years before you plan to stop working. Starting ten years earlier can roughly halve the monthly investment you need. If you are already in your 50s, a plan still matters: it decides how to structure the corpus you have so it lasts.

How much money do I need to retire in India?

A common starting point is 25 to 30 times your annual expenses in the year you retire. For example, a family spending ₹2 lakh a month today and retiring in 15 years at 6% inflation will spend about ₹4.8 lakh a month at retirement, which needs a corpus of roughly ₹13 crore for a 30-year retirement earning 8% a year.

Is ₹5 crore enough to retire in India?

It depends on your expenses and how early you retire. At a sustainable withdrawal rate of 3% to 3.5%, ₹5 crore supports about ₹1.25 lakh to ₹1.45 lakh a month in the first year of retirement, rising with inflation. That can suit a modest lifestyle, but is usually too little for an HNI family in a metro or for someone retiring before 55.

What is a safe withdrawal rate in India?

Many Indian planners use 3% to 3.5% a year rather than the 4% rule common in the US, because inflation in India is higher and retirements that start at 55 can last 35 years or more. The right rate depends on your asset mix, other income such as rent, and how flexible your spending is.

How much of my NPS can I withdraw at retirement?

Under PFRDA's December 2025 amendments, non-government subscribers can withdraw up to 80% of their NPS corpus as a lump sum at normal exit and must use at least 20% to buy an annuity when the corpus is above ₹12 lakh. Smaller corpuses have more flexible options, and withdrawal can be deferred up to age 85. Government subscribers remain at 60:40.

Is the NPS lump sum withdrawal tax-free?

As of September 2026, 60% of the NPS corpus withdrawn at retirement is tax-exempt. The additional 20% now allowed for non-government subscribers has not been clearly exempted and may be taxed at your slab rate, and annuity income is taxable every year. Check the current position before you withdraw.

What is the bucket strategy for retirement?

The bucket strategy divides a retirement corpus by when you will need the money. Bucket 1 holds 2 to 3 years of expenses in safe, liquid instruments, bucket 2 holds the next 5 to 7 years in bonds and debt funds, and bucket 3 holds long-term money in equity. You spend from bucket 1 and refill it from the others, so a market fall never forces you to sell equity at a loss.

How is retirement income taxed in India?

It depends on the source. Pension and annuity income, FD and SCSS interest, and gains on debt funds bought on or after 1 April 2023 are taxed at your slab rate. Long-term gains on listed shares and equity mutual funds are taxed at 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year, so a systematic withdrawal plan from equity funds is often more tax-efficient than interest income.

How often should I review my retirement plan?

At least once a year, and whenever something significant changes: a business sale, an ESOP vesting, a move abroad or back to India, a health event or a change in tax rules. Reviews check that the corpus is on track and refill or rebalance the buckets.

Should I use a SEBI-registered investment adviser for retirement planning?

A SEBI-registered investment adviser is regulated to act in your interest and must disclose how they are paid, which matters when a plan covers your entire savings. You can verify any adviser's registration on SEBI's website. Fintoo is registered with SEBI as an investment adviser, registration no. INA000020031.

How should NRIs plan to retire in India?

NRIs returning to India should plan for residential status (including RNOR), repatriation rules, taxation of foreign pensions and retirement accounts, currency risk, and which assets to hold in India versus abroad. These are best planned together, several years before the move.
