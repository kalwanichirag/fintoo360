What is a mutual fund, and how does it work?

A mutual fund collects money from thousands of investors and puts it to work in a single, diversified portfolio. A professional fund manager decides what to buy and sell, following the strategy stated in the scheme's documents. You receive units in proportion to what you invest, and their value rises or falls with the portfolio.

India's mutual fund industry manages over ₹87 lakh crore, and households now invest more than ₹32,000 crore every month through SIPs (AMFI, August 2026). Every fund is set up as a trust, overseen by trustees, with assets held by an independent custodian, so the fund house never holds your money itself.

How your money moves

  1. 01

    You invest

    A lump sum or a monthly SIP, from your own bank account.

  2. 02

    Money is pooled

    With thousands of other investors, into one scheme.

  3. 03

    Manager invests

    In shares, bonds, gold or other assets the scheme allows.

  4. 04

    You own units

    Valued daily at the NAV, sold back to the fund when you need the money.

Six terms worth knowing

NAV
Net asset value: the price of one unit, worked out at the end of each business day.
Expense ratio
The yearly cost of running the fund, taken from its assets. Lower is better.
Exit load
A charge some funds apply if you redeem within a set period, often one year.
Riskometer
SEBI's six-level risk label, from Low to Very High, shown on every scheme.
Benchmark
The index a fund is measured against, such as the Nifty 100 for large-cap funds.
AUM
Assets under management: the total value of money the scheme manages.

Why invest in mutual funds, and what are the risks?

Mutual funds make investing simple, affordable and diversified. But returns are not guaranteed. Here is the good and the not-so-good, at a glance.

+Why people invest

  • Spread your risk

    One fund holds dozens of companies.

  • Experts manage it

    Professionals pick and track the investments.

  • Easy to withdraw

    Money usually back in 1 to 3 days.

  • Start small

    Monthly SIPs from a few hundred rupees.

  • Regulated by SEBI

    Strict rules on costs and disclosure.

  • Tax-friendly

    Tax only when you sell.

!Risks to keep in mind

  • Prices go up and downEquity funds can fall sharply in a bad year.
  • Interest rates changeDebt funds can dip when rates rise.
  • Borrowers can defaultLower-rated bonds carry more risk.
  • Panic selling hurts mostSelling in a fall locks in the loss.

The fix for most of these: match the fund to how long you can stay invested. The table below shows how.

What are the types of mutual funds in India?

SEBI groups mutual funds by what they invest in. Equity funds buy shares for long-term growth, debt funds lend to governments and companies for stability, hybrid funds mix the two, and passive and other funds track an index or hold gold, global shares or a blend that changes with your age.

Which mutual fund for which goal?

The simplest rule: the sooner you need the money, the safer the fund should be. Money needed within three years belongs mostly in debt or arbitrage funds. Money you will not touch for five years or more can go into equity, which has time to recover from falls.

How do you choose the right mutual fund?

Start with your goal, not with last year's top performer. Pick the category first, then the fund within it, judging consistency, cost and process rather than recent returns. Star ratings and "best fund" lists are a starting point at most, because they look backwards.

  1. 01

    Match the fund to the goal

    When you need the money decides how much equity you can hold. A house deposit in two years and retirement in twenty need very different funds.

  2. 02

    Check consistency, not peaks

    Look at rolling returns over five years or more against the benchmark and category. A fund that does reasonably well most of the time often beats one that tops the chart once.

  3. 03

    See how it behaves in falls

    Downside capture and the worst year tell you what holding the fund will feel like. That matters more than the best year.

  4. 04

    Keep costs low

    Choose the direct plan, and compare expense ratios within the category. In index funds, cost and tracking error are almost everything.

  5. 05

    Understand the process and the people

    A clear, repeatable investment style and a stable team matter. A change of fund manager is a reason to review, not automatically to exit.

  6. 06

    Look inside the portfolio

    For debt funds, check credit quality and duration. For equity funds, check concentration and overlap with funds you already own.

  7. 07

    Know the exit rules

    Exit loads, lock-ins and the tax on redemption shape when and how you can leave. Read them before you invest, not when you need the money.

SIP or lump sum: how should you invest?

A systematic investment plan (SIP) invests a fixed amount every month, so you buy more units when prices are low and fewer when they are high. A lump sum works when you already have the money and a long horizon. Raising your SIP each year with your income, a step-up, often matters more than the fund you pick.

How are mutual funds taxed in India?

You pay tax only when you redeem or switch units, or when you receive a payout. The rate depends on the type of fund and how long you held it. Equity funds held for over a year get the lowest rate, while most debt funds bought after 1 April 2023 are taxed at your slab rate whatever the holding period.

Direct vs regular mutual funds: what is the difference?

Every scheme has two versions with the same portfolio and the same fund manager. The regular plan pays a commission to whoever sold it to you, taken from your returns every year through a higher expense ratio. The direct plan pays no commission, so more of the return stays with you.

Common mutual fund mistakes, and what to do instead

Most mutual fund investors earn less than the funds they own, because of when they buy and sell. A few habits avoid most of the damage.

  • 01

    Chasing last year's winner

    Instead: pick funds for consistency over five years and how they fit your portfolio.

  • 02

    Owning 20 funds that hold the same stocks

    Instead: five to seven funds with distinct roles usually give all the diversification you need.

  • 03

    Stopping SIPs when markets fall

    Instead: keep going. Falls are when a SIP buys the most units for the same money.

  • 04

    Buying an NFO because the NAV is ₹10

    Instead: ignore the NAV level. A ₹10 NAV is no cheaper than a ₹500 one; only the portfolio matters.

  • 05

    Putting short-term money in equity

    Instead: money needed within three years belongs in liquid, short-term debt or arbitrage funds.

  • 06

    Forgetting to rebalance

    Instead: review once a year, and bring your equity and debt back to target using new money first.

What changed for mutual fund investors in 2025 and 2026?

SEBI rewrote the mutual fund rulebook for the first time since 1996 and reorganised fund categories. Most changes lower costs or make funds easier to compare, but a few affect which funds you can hold.

  • New Mutual Funds Regulations, 2026

    From 1 April 2026, costs are shown as a base expense ratio, with taxes such as GST and STT charged separately on actuals. Expense caps for index funds, ETFs and close-ended schemes came down, and brokerage limits were cut.

  • Life cycle funds replace children's and retirement funds

    A new category that moves from equity to safer assets along a glide path as its target year approaches. Solution-oriented schemes are being folded in.

  • Less copy-paste between funds

    Sectoral and thematic funds can overlap no more than 50% with a fund house's other equity schemes, and a fund house can now run both a value and a contra fund if they overlap by no more than 50%.

  • ₹250 "Chhoti SIPs"

    Fund houses now offer SIPs from ₹250 a month in selected schemes, making first-time investing easier.

  • Up to 10 nominees per folio

    Since March 2025 you can name up to 10 nominees on each mutual fund folio, with the share for each.

Mutual fund FAQs

Clear answers to the questions first-time and experienced investors ask us most.

Last reviewed 1 October 2026 by Mihir Shah, AVP, Financial Planning, Fintoo.

What is a mutual fund?

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it in shares, bonds, gold or other assets according to a stated strategy, managed by a professional fund manager and regulated by SEBI. Each investor owns units, and the value of each unit, the NAV, rises or falls with the value of the portfolio.

Are mutual funds safe?

Mutual funds are tightly regulated: each fund is a trust overseen by trustees, assets are held by an independent custodian, and SEBI sets rules on holdings, costs and disclosure. But they are not guaranteed. Equity funds can fall sharply in bad years and debt funds carry interest-rate and credit risk, so the right fund depends on your horizon.

How do I start investing in mutual funds?

Complete a one-time KYC with your PAN and Aadhaar, decide your goal and how long you can stay invested, choose a fund category and then a fund, and invest through a SIP or a lump sum, preferably in the direct plan. With Fintoo, an advisor helps you choose and builds the portfolio in direct plans at zero commission.

What is the minimum amount to invest in a mutual fund?

Most funds accept SIPs from ₹100 to ₹500 a month and lump sums from ₹500 to ₹5,000. Many fund houses now also offer ₹250 Chhoti SIPs in selected schemes. Specialised Investment Funds, a separate category for larger investors, need at least ₹10 lakh.

Is SIP better than lump sum investment?

A SIP suits investing from monthly income and averages your purchase price over time, which reduces the risk of investing everything at a market peak. A lump sum can earn more when you already have the money and a long horizon. For large sums, a systematic transfer plan from a liquid fund into equity over 6 to 12 months is a common middle path.

What is the difference between direct and regular mutual funds?

Both versions hold the same portfolio with the same manager. The regular plan pays an annual commission to the distributor through a higher expense ratio, often 0.5 to 1% a year more. The direct plan has no commission, so its returns are higher by that difference every year.

Does a lower NAV mean a mutual fund is cheaper?

No. The NAV only reflects the value of the fund's holdings divided by its units. A fund with a ₹10 NAV is not cheaper than one with a ₹500 NAV; what matters is the quality of the portfolio, its cost and how it fits your goals. This is why buying a new fund offer just for its ₹10 NAV makes no sense.

How are mutual fund returns taxed in India?

Equity fund gains held over 12 months are taxed at 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year, and at 20% if held for less. Debt funds bought on or after 1 April 2023 are taxed at your slab rate. Gold, international and some hybrid funds are taxed at 12.5% after 24 months. Payouts are taxed at your slab rate.

What is ELSS and how does it save tax?

An Equity Linked Savings Scheme is a diversified equity fund with a three-year lock-in. Investments of up to ₹1.5 lakh a year qualify for the deduction formerly called Section 80C, now Section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, but only if you use the old tax regime. Gains are taxed like other equity funds.

What is a systematic withdrawal plan (SWP)?

A systematic withdrawal plan pays you a fixed amount from your mutual fund every month or quarter by redeeming units. It is often used for retirement income because only the gain portion of each withdrawal is taxed, and the rest of the money stays invested.

Can NRIs invest in Indian mutual funds?

Yes. NRIs can invest through NRE or NRO accounts after completing KYC and FATCA declarations. Investments through NRE accounts can be repatriated. Some fund houses restrict investors resident in the US and Canada because of local reporting rules, so the choice of funds may be narrower.

How many mutual funds should I own?

For most investors, five to seven funds are enough: for example a large or flexi cap fund, a mid or small cap fund, an international fund, one or two debt funds and gold. More funds usually add overlap and paperwork, not diversification.

Can I withdraw my mutual fund money at any time?

In open-ended funds, yes. Equity fund redemptions are usually paid within one to three working days and liquid funds the next day. ELSS has a three-year lock-in, some funds charge an exit load if you redeem early, and the redemption may be taxable.

What is a life cycle fund?

A life cycle fund is a new SEBI category from 2026 that invests across equity, debt, gold and other assets and gradually shifts towards safer assets as a target year approaches, following a glide path. It replaces the older children's and retirement solution-oriented funds.
