What is an SWP?

A Systematic Withdrawal Plan lets you invest a lumpsum once and then withdraw a fixed amount from it at regular intervals — usually monthly — while the rest stays invested and keeps earning returns. It's commonly used to turn a retirement corpus into a regular income stream.

Whether the balance grows or shrinks over time depends entirely on how your withdrawal rate compares to your returns: withdraw less than the corpus earns and it keeps growing; withdraw more, and it draws down — potentially to zero, as this calculator will show you.

How this calculator works

Bt  =  Bt−1 × (1 + r) − W

Bt — balance this month Bt−1 — last month's balance r — monthly rate equivalent to the annual return W — monthly withdrawal

The annual return is converted to its equivalent compounded monthly rate, and each withdrawal is applied at month-end. This repeats every month, so the calculator simulates it month by month.

Common questions

What if I withdraw more than my returns?

Your balance draws down faster than it earns, and can run out before your time period ends — exactly what the warning badge above flags when it happens for your numbers.

Is SWP income taxable?

Each withdrawal is a partial redemption of fund units, so only the gain portion is taxed as capital gains — not the whole amount. Our Capital Gains Tax Calculator works out the LTCG/STCG on that portion.

Can I change or pause the withdrawal?

Yes — most SWPs can be modified, paused or stopped at any time directly with the fund house, subject to that scheme's own exit load window.

Is SWP better than an FD for regular income?

Neither is simply better. An FD gives a fixed, guaranteed rate with fully taxable interest each year. An SWP draws from a market-linked investment, so both the returns and your remaining principal can fluctuate.
